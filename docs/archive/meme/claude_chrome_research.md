The "Permanent Underclass" Meme — Deep Dive Analysis
What It Is
The "permanent underclass" is a viral meme/concept that has taken over Tech Twitter (now X), particularly within the AI, crypto, and startup communities. It revolves around the anxiety that AI and technology are about to create an irreversible economic divide — and that there is a closing window of time to "make it" (get rich, build something, acquire assets) before you are permanently locked out of wealth and relevance.
The meme typically takes the form: "You have X years to escape the permanent underclass" — sometimes serious, sometimes ironic, sometimes both.

Origins & Key Figures
The phrase "permanent underclass" existed long before this meme (it has roots in political science and sociology — Gunnar Myrdal's concept of an economic underclass trapped by structural forces). But its current viral iteration on Twitter is firmly tied to the AI acceleration era of 2024–2025.
The single most viral post that crystallized the meme came from roon (@tszzl), a well-known AI/tech personality, on August 5, 2024:

"dad why are we poor? well son there was a minor market panic and i sold everything right before the technological singularity and now we're part of the permanent underclass"

This post got 5.8K likes, 353 reposts, and 402K+ views — massive numbers that helped cement the phrase into the tech Twitter lexicon. Roon has posted about it multiple times since, including a Jan 20, 2025 post noting it seems bizarre people actually believe in the concept, and a Jan 30 post using it satirically, and even a Feb 6, 2026 post declaring it "cancelled."

How It Spread
The meme spread through several overlapping waves:
Wave 1 — The AI anxiety context (mid-2024): As AI capabilities accelerated rapidly (GPT-4, Claude, etc.), Silicon Valley insiders began talking about how AI would concentrate wealth among a few. The idea that you needed to position yourself now — buy Nvidia stock, build an AI startup, get equity — or be left behind forever became a recurring theme. Roon's Aug 2024 post was the crystallizing moment.
Wave 2 — Startup/builder culture adoption (late 2024–2025): The phrase was adopted by the "builder bro" and hustle culture communities. People like Ayush S (@ayushswrites) used it as a motivational rallying cry — his Sep 4, 2025 post declared the final months of 2025 the last chance to escape. Codie Sanchez (@Codie_Sanchez) amplified the ownership-class angle in a viral Feb 14 post about equity vs. salary. Accounts literally rebranded — there's now an account called "ESCAPE THE PERMANENT UNDERCLASS" (@incelscorp) and even a Solana token called "escape the permanent underclass" (@underclassSOL).
Wave 3 — Ironic/meme adoption (2025–2026): The phrase became so ubiquitous that it became a self-aware meme. People started using it ironically: @uncledoomer compared modern hooded tunics to medieval serfs' clothing. @paularambles joked about an SF escape room called "The Permanent Underclass" that's just a room with a laptop and Claude Code installed. @nearcyan posted a video about not buying Nvidia stock. @0xAndros joked about escaping it with AI coding tools (Codex, Claude Code, Devin). Someone even put it in Tejas Bhakta's bio — a former Tesla ML engineer who describes himself as escaping it.
Wave 4 — Mainstream discourse and backlash (early 2026): The meme reached mainstream discourse. Scott Alexander wrote a major Astral Codex Ten piece on Jan 1, 2026 examining the concept critically. The New Yorker's Kyle Chayka explored it. Hacker News is actively debating it as of today. People at AI frontier labs are being satirized for saying it with a straight face while also wondering why the general public despises them.

Why It Resonates
The meme draws its power from several converging anxieties:
1. AI displacement fear: The genuine concern that AI will automate away most jobs, concentrating wealth among those who own the AI systems and the companies deploying them.
2. The "ownership class" divide: The idea (amplified by figures like Codie Sanchez) that the world is splitting into two classes — those who own equity/assets and those who work for salary — and AI will make this divide permanent and irreversible.
3. Urgency and FOMO: The meme creates a ticking clock. "You have 2 years." "4 months." "X years." This urgency drives engagement, hustle culture content, and startup activity. It's both motivational and anxiety-inducing.
4. Crypto/tech bubble psychology: Having missed Bitcoin, missed Nvidia stock, missed the AI startup wave — these are the "sins" that consign you to the underclass. The meme captures the regret of not acting.
5. Historical resonance: It taps into genuine sociological concepts about structural poverty traps, but repackages them for the tech/AI era.

The Criticism & Counter-Meme
Not everyone buys it. There's a strong counter-current:

Roon himself has oscillated between propagating and dismissing it (calling it "cancelled" in Feb 2026)
@somefoundersalt satirized AI frontier lab people who say "You only have X years to escape" while insisting they'll be in the "permanent overclass"
@ktrivs flipped it politically: "if you don't have 'funded by Peter Thiel' allegations in 2026, congratulations you are now part of the permanent underclass"
Scott Alexander's analysis reportedly examines why the "get rich while you still can" plan doesn't hold up if we're assuming basic rules of capitalism change
Critics note it functions as marketing for hustle culture, crypto schemes, AI courses, and startup accelerators


Current Status (Feb 2026)
The meme is extremely active right now — posts are coming in every few minutes. It has fully permeated tech Twitter culture to the point where it's simultaneously used sincerely (by anxious builders), ironically (by shitposters), commercially (by people selling courses/tokens), and critically (by cultural commentators). It's become one of the defining memes of the AI acceleration era, encapsulating both genuine economic anxiety and the absurdity of Silicon Valley's relationship with wealth and status.
